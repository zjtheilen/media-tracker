# Phase 1 — Identity Catalog

**Project:** Media Tracker
**Authoritative branch:** `develop-3`
**Phase:** Phase 1 — Intelligence Alignment
**Status:** Historical conceptual development record — superseded by the Phase 1 Identity Fixture Contract

> **Authority:** This document preserves the historical development of the Identity catalog. It is not the current implementation authority. Where this document conflicts with the current Identity Fixture Contract, accepted fixture definitions, or implemented Phase 1 behavior, those later artifacts take precedence.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Identity layer describes the **broader curatorial philosophy expressed by an archive**.

An Identity is not simply another name for a Designation, nor is it a personality diagnosis.

The intended Identity question is:

> **What relationship does this curator repeatedly establish with the qualities, ideas, and experiences represented in their archive?**

A Designation describes a recognizable **taste classification**.

An Identity describes a broader **curatorial philosophy or mode of engagement** synthesized from multiple signals.

This distinction became foundational to the Phase 1 Identity work.

The original purpose of this document was to explore and develop possible Identity concepts before implementation changes were made. That exploratory work is now historical. The accepted Identity ontology was subsequently differentiated, specified, mapped to evidence, implemented through fixtures, and protected by tests.

---

# 2. Governing Principles

## 2.1 Identity is not a renamed Designation

An Identity should not be derivable by simply:

- renaming a Designation
- reweighting the same Designation signals
- restating a Designation's description
- describing the same taste pattern with more abstract language

Shared evidence is acceptable.

Shared conclusions are not.

The critical Phase 1 rule became:

> **Evidence can overlap. Meaning cannot.**

---

## 2.2 Identity is not personality diagnosis

Identity describes observable or reasonably inferred patterns in the archive.

It must not claim that the user:

- has a particular psychological personality type
- possesses a clinical characteristic
- has a fixed personal identity
- behaves in ways the archive cannot demonstrate
- has characteristics that exist independently of the archive

The system describes the **archive**, not the entirety of the person.

---

## 2.3 Identity should synthesize multiple signals

An Identity represents a coherent combination of signals.

A single high trait score should generally contribute toward an Identity rather than independently defining one.

This does not mean every Identity must use many unrelated signals. It means an Identity should represent a meaningful conceptual pattern rather than simply promoting an individual metric into an archetype.

---

## 2.4 Evidence and interpretation are distinct

The same underlying evidence may legitimately contribute to both Designations and Identities when the systems draw different conclusions from it.

For example:

- originality may support a Designation describing experimental taste
- originality may also support an Identity describing a relationship with unfamiliar territory

The distinction comes from the **meaning of the conclusion**, not necessarily from completely separate raw data.

---

## 2.5 Do not force a fixed number of Identities

The historical catalog exploration intentionally did not assume a permanent number of Identities.

A viable Identity must be:

1. meaningfully distinct,
2. supported by available evidence,
3. explainable from the archive,
4. useful to the archive narrative,
5. distinguishable from Designations and other Identities.

The current Phase 1 catalog contains three accepted concepts. Future Identity concepts remain possible without implying that the current catalog is permanently closed.

# 3. Evidence Landscape

The Identity work considered several broad categories of archive evidence.

## 3.1 Appreciation

Examples include:

- craft
- originality
- depth
- emotional impact
- engagement
- presentation
- pacing

These describe qualities the curator tends to value.

They are useful evidence but do not automatically define Identity.

---

## 3.2 Taste Orientation

Examples include:

- experimental affinity
- genre prevalence
- novelty
- gameplay mechanics
- genre diversity

These primarily describe what kinds of experiences the archive favors.

This is often Designation territory, although some of these signals may support broader Identity concepts when interpreted differently.

---

## 3.3 Interpretive Orientation

Examples include:

- analysis
- ambiguity
- reflection
- depth
- psychological prevalence
- mystery prevalence
- surreal or uncertain experiences

These provide some of the strongest evidence for the Interpretive Identity because they can describe a relationship with media rather than merely a preference for a particular category.

---

## 3.4 Archive Structure

Examples include:

- genre diversity
- media-type breadth
- archive size
- genre concentration
- distribution of ratings
- distribution of media types

Archive structure can provide strong evidence about observable range.

It does **not** automatically establish intentionality.

The distinction is:

> **The archive is broad.**

versus:

> **The curator deliberately seeks breadth.**

The first is observable.

The second is an inference.

---

# 4. Historical Candidate Development

The original Identity exploration produced several candidates.

These candidates were useful for testing the conceptual boundary of Identity, but they were not automatically promoted into the final catalog.

The major historical candidates included:

- Interpretive Philosophy
- Exploratory Philosophy
- Construction / Systems Philosophy
- Breadth / Curatorial Variety Philosophy

Earlier Identity vocabulary also included concepts whose names and descriptions substantially overlapped existing Designations:

- Boundary Explorer
- Deep Diver
- Engagement Architect

The Phase 1 differentiation work established that these overlapping Identity concepts should not simply survive under their existing names.

A rename alone would not solve the conceptual duplication.

# 5. Interpretive Philosophy

## Historical status

**Strong conceptual survivor**

Interpretive Philosophy became one of the three accepted Phase 1 Identity concepts.

## Concept

An Interpretive Identity describes a curator whose relationship with media is characterized by:

- depth
- reflection
- complexity
- ambiguity
- interpretation
- meaning-making

The archive suggests that experiences are valued not only for what they are, but for what they invite the curator to think about, unpack, or reconsider.

## Core question

> **How do you engage with what you consume?**

More specifically:

> **Does this archive repeatedly engage with media as something to interpret, unpack, and reconsider?**

## What it means

Interpretive Philosophy may be expressed through combinations of:

- depth
- emotional impact
- reflection
- ambiguity
- analysis
- layered narratives
- psychologically rich experiences
- uncertain or multi-interpretive experiences

The emphasis is on **interpretation and meaning**, rather than simply liking difficult or complicated media.

## What it does not mean

It does not mean:

- the curator prefers psychological media
- the curator prefers mystery
- the curator likes complicated stories
- the curator is intellectually superior
- the curator is analytical in every area of life
- the curator has a particular personality type

Those would be narrower taste observations or unsupported personal conclusions.

## Relationship to Designation

A Deep Diver Designation describes a recognizable taste pattern toward depth and layered experiences.

Interpretive Philosophy describes the broader relationship with those experiences:

> **The curator tends to engage with media as something to interpret, question, and unpack.**

The Designation answers:

> **What do you tend to like?**

The Identity answers:

> **What relationship do you tend to establish with what you like?**

## Evidence limitation

The archive does not directly observe thoughts, conversations, reviews, or post-consumption reflection.

Therefore Interpretive Philosophy remains an evidence-based inference from repeated archive signals rather than a direct measurement of internal cognition.

---

# 6. Exploratory Philosophy

## Historical status

**Conditional conceptual survivor; accepted into the Phase 1 catalog with explicit evidence limitations**

## Concept

Exploratory Philosophy describes a curator whose archive demonstrates a relationship with:

- novelty
- unfamiliarity
- contrast
- boundary expansion
- experiences outside established territory

The important distinction is that exploration is more than liking experimental media.

It concerns a broader relationship with **unfamiliar territory**.

## Core question

> **How do you relate to the boundaries of what you consume?**

More specifically:

> **Does this archive repeatedly demonstrate engagement with experiences beyond established taste territory?**

## What it means

Relevant evidence can include:

- originality
- genre diversity
- depth
- experimental affinity
- novelty

These signals describe characteristics associated with exploratory behavior.

## What it does not mean

It does not mean:

- the curator simply likes experimental media
- one unusual work proves exploration
- the curator enjoys everything unconventional
- the curator is adventurous in life generally
- the archive proves deliberate exploration

## Relationship to Designation

A Boundary Explorer Designation is a recognizable taste classification centered on boundary-pushing or unfamiliar media.

Exploratory Philosophy is broader:

> **The curator demonstrates a recurring relationship with unfamiliar or boundary-expanding territory.**

The two concepts may legitimately coexist, but neither should be defined as a simple restatement of the other.

## Evidence limitation

The current archive observes preference characteristics more directly than it observes exploration itself.

It does not directly observe:

- intent
- deliberate search behavior
- movement through taste space
- exploration trajectory

Therefore Exploratory Philosophy must not be presented as proof of deliberate exploration.

# 7. Construction / Systems Philosophy

## Historical status

**Deferred**

Construction / Systems Philosophy was a useful conceptual candidate but did not survive as an initial Phase 1 Identity.

## Concept explored

The candidate described a curator whose archive demonstrated appreciation for:

- construction
- structure
- pacing
- mechanics
- systems
- execution
- craft

## Why it was considered

The concept appeared capable of representing a meaningful relationship with how experiences are constructed rather than merely whether they are engaging.

## Why it was deferred

The available evidence was too closely coupled to the existing Engagement Architect Designation.

In particular, current `system_design` evidence is strongly derived from gameplay mechanics.

That means the available evidence did not establish a sufficiently independent Construction / Systems philosophy.

The Phase 1 differentiation audit therefore deferred the concept rather than forcing it into the catalog.

## Future status

Construction / Systems Philosophy may be reconsidered if future archive evidence provides stronger independent signals about appreciation of construction or systems.

No such evidence should be invented merely to revive the concept.

---

# 8. Breadth Philosophy

## Historical status

**Conditional conceptual survivor; accepted into the Phase 1 catalog**

## Concept

Breadth Philosophy describes the **range of territory represented in the archive**.

Its emphasis is on archive composition rather than a particular favorite.

## Core question

> **How wide is the territory you consume?**

More specifically:

> **How much variety does the archive demonstrably contain?**

## What it means

Observable evidence includes:

- genre diversity
- representation across the media landscape
- archive composition
- distribution across different categories

The strongest current observable signal is genre diversity.

## What it does not mean

It does not automatically mean:

- the curator deliberately seeks variety
- the curator is organized
- the curator is a collector
- the curator is broadly curious in every domain
- the curator intentionally diversifies their archive

Those are stronger claims than the available evidence supports.

## Relationship to Designation

The Curator Designation describes a recognizable taste classification involving the shape and quality of an archive.

Breadth Philosophy describes the observable **range of territory** represented by that archive.

The distinction remains important:

> **Breadth measures range. It does not prove intention.**

# 9. Future Identity Concepts

The historical exploration also identified concepts that may become valuable later.

## 9.1 Connection / Synthesis

A curator who repeatedly connects:

- ideas
- genres
- media
- themes
- experiences

into broader patterns.

Current limitation:

The archive does not yet capture enough explicit cross-entry relationships to establish this reliably.

Potential future evidence could include:

- user-written notes
- thematic links
- explicit comparisons
- recurring themes
- cross-media relationships

**Status:** Future candidate.

---

## 9.2 Re-engagement / Revisitation

A curator whose relationship with media includes:

- rereading
- replaying
- revisiting
- returning to previous experiences

This represents a fundamentally different relationship with media than simple preference.

Current limitation:

The archive does not yet sufficiently model explicit revisitation behavior.

**Status:** Future candidate.

---

# 10. Concepts That Should Not Automatically Become Identities

Several concepts are better represented by Traits, Genre Signals, or Designations.

## Experimental Taste

Experimental affinity is useful evidence.

Experimental preference alone is primarily a taste orientation and therefore generally belongs in Designation territory.

## Deep Taste

Strong depth preference describes a recognizable taste pattern.

It should not automatically become an Identity.

## Engagement Taste

Strong engagement preference describes a recognizable taste pattern.

It should not automatically become an Identity.

## Emotional Immersion

Emotional impact may contribute to an Identity.

Emotional preference by itself does not establish a broader Identity.

# 11. Relationship to Designations

The Identity and Designation layers remain related but non-equivalent.

| Dimension                 | Designation                                               | Identity                                                          |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Core question             | What recognizable taste classification fits this archive? | What broader curatorial philosophy does this archive demonstrate? |
| Subject                   | Taste pattern                                             | Mode of curation / engagement                                     |
| Primary evidence          | Traits, genres, archive shape                             | Synthesized patterns across multiple signals                      |
| Output                    | Recognizable archive classification                       | Broader curatorial philosophy                                     |
| Shared evidence           | Allowed                                                   | Allowed                                                           |
| Shared conclusion         | Not allowed                                               | Not allowed                                                       |
| Shared name               | Forbidden as a conceptual duplicate                       | Forbidden as a conceptual duplicate                               |
| Personality diagnosis     | No                                                        | No                                                                |
| Score                     | Yes                                                       | Yes                                                               |
| Data Sufficiency          | Applicable where required                                 | Applicable                                                        |
| Classification Confidence | Future / optional                                         | Future / optional                                                 |

The two systems may consume overlapping evidence.

They must not become two names for the same conclusion.

# 13. Retired Identity Concepts

The following previous Identity concepts are retired as Identity definitions:

- Boundary Explorer
- Deep Diver
- Engagement Architect

These concepts remain valid as Designations.

Their retirement from Identity does not mean that the underlying evidence was discarded.

The same evidence may continue to contribute to the new Identity concepts where the interpretation is different.

# 14. Current Implementation Relationship

The accepted Identity catalog is implemented through fixture-driven definitions.

The current architecture preserves:

- fixture-driven Identity loading
- weighted Identity scoring
- trait resolution
- derived-trait resolution
- normalized contributions
- contribution breakdowns
- deterministic ranking
- primary Identity selection
- secondary Identity presentation
- Data Sufficiency requirements
- recommendation-bias metadata
- explanation infrastructure

The catalog migration did not require replacing this architecture.

The implementation principle is:

> **Change the Identity concepts without unnecessarily rewriting the machinery that evaluates them.**

---

# 15. Historical Significance

This document should remain available because the discarded candidates explain important design decisions.

In particular, the historical material demonstrates why:

- Identity cannot simply duplicate Designation
- experimental taste is not automatically exploration
- depth is not automatically interpretation
- archive breadth is not proof of intentional diversification
- systems appreciation requires stronger evidence than gameplay mechanics alone
- future Identity concepts should be rejected or deferred when the archive cannot explain them

These conclusions informed the current Identity Fixture Contract and evidence mapping.
